# GreyTimeTravel Research Note

## Main Topic
The paper explores the philosophical and metaphysical issues surrounding the concept of time travel, particularly backward time travel. The author, William Grey, discusses the challenges and paradoxes that arise from the possibility of traversing time, focusing on the Parmenidean view of the world as a four-dimensional manifold of events.

## Core Claims
1. Time travel requires a four-dimensional ontology where the world is a manifold of events and individuals are aggregates of temporal stages ('timelike streaks').
2. The Heraclitean metaphysical conception, which holds that the future is genuinely open, rules out travel to the future because there is simply nowhere to go.
3. Travel into the past would make it accessible in a way that would allow the traveler to change things, which is contradictory.
4. Reverse causation may not be contradictory but is certainly problematic in the extreme, as something that does not yet exist could now exert a causal influence.

## Important Concepts
- Four-dimensional manifold of events
- Temporal stages or 'timelike streaks'
- Reverse causation
- Double occupancy problem
- Personal time vs. external time
- Counterfactual dependence

## Assumptions
- The world we live in is a Parmenidean world: a four-dimensional manifold of events.
- The occupants of the world are four-dimensional aggregates of stages ('timelike streaks').

## Open Questions
1. How can the 'movement' into the past possibly get started without a collision between the time machine going into the past and the time traveler's stages in the future?
2. Is an ontology of individuals extended in time as well as in space defensible?
3. How can we reconcile the difference in our attitude to the future compared to the past, and the feeling that future times contain genuine alternatives which past times lack?

## Useful For Discovery AI
The research note provides a comprehensive overview of the philosophical and metaphysical challenges associated with time travel, which could be useful for Discovery AI in understanding the limitations and implications of creating time-traveling agents or characters in simulated worlds. The concepts of four-dimensional manifolds, temporal stages, and reverse causation could be particularly relevant for AI researchers working on developing models of time and causality in their simulations.
